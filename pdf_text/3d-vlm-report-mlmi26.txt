Abstract. While medical vision-language models (VLMs) show promise
for automated radiology report generation, existing approaches typically
rely on 2D encoders that discard essential volumetric data. Furthermore,
they universally assume a single scan per patient, overlooking the multi-
study imaging common in longitudinal clinical care. In this paper, we
propose a unified native 3D VLM framework designed for structured
multi-study CT and MRI report generation. Our dual-projector archi-
tecture bridges specialized 3D vision encoders with a clinically adapted
language model through a contrastively grounded intermediate semantic
space. To prevent the degradation of vision-text representations during
fine-tuning, we introduce a joint generative-contrastive objective that
maintains contrastive alignment across all training stages. Additionally,
we implement and evaluate distinct aggregation strategies to effectively
process patients with multiple historical imaging studies. Evaluated on
the Penn Medicine BioBank (9,384 patients; 29,523 scans), our frame-
work outperforms existing 2D and 3D medical VLMs across standard lex-
ical metrics and multi-dimensional clinical assessments spanning anatom-
ical coverage, critical findings, diagnostic plausibility, and report quality.

Introduction
Radiology report generation directly guides patient care, yet drafting reports
is cognitively complex and high-stakes: clinicians must synthesize 3D anatomy,
detect subtle abnormalities across slices, and translate visual evidence into stan-
dardized text. As imaging volumes outpace the available radiology workforce [22],

automated medical vision-language models (VLMs) offer a scalable way to re-
duce clinician burden. More broadly, clinical NLP studies confirm the value of
such task-specific adaptation: MentalGPT [29] instruction-tunes LLMs for em-
pathetic mental-health support, MentalChat16K [30] supplies domain-specific
dialogues for training and evaluation, and DALK [11] grounds biomedical ques-
tion answering in a literature-derived knowledge graph. None of these, however,
address structured, image-grounded radiology reporting. Healthcare VLMs built
for imaging, such as LLaVA-Med [10] and Med-Flamingo [16], rely mainly on
2D encoders such as CLIP [21], while treating volumes as independent slices, as
in prior slice-based adaptations [4], discards spatial continuity essential for CT
and MRI interpretation. Native 3D VLMs, including CT2Rep [6], RadFM [26],
E3D-GPT [9], and Med3DVLM [28], narrow this gap, but key limitations re-
main. First, they pair spatial encoders directly with general-purpose language
models (e.g., Vicuna [5], LLaMA [23]) that lack clinical grounding. Second, they
confine contrastive alignment [32, 13] to pretraining and discard it during genera-
tive fine-tuning, allowing vision-text representations to degrade as the model fits
language patterns—a phenomenon we term clinical drift. Finally, existing sys-
tems assume a single study and cannot aggregate the longitudinal or multi-phase
scans common in clinical practice.
In this paper, we present a unified, native 3D vision-language framework for
structured CT and MRI report generation that departs from prior approaches
which retrofit 2D architectures to volumetric data. Instead, we (i) introduce a 3D
dual-projector framework that couples state-of-the-art volumetric encoders (CT-
FM [18] and Merlin [3]) with a domain-adapted clinical language model (MMed-
Llama-3 [20]) within a clinically grounded ClinicalBERT [1] space. Building on
this aligned representation, we (ii) formulate a joint Generative-InfoNCE ob-
jective [17] that sustains cross-modal consistency during instruction tuning. To
move beyond the single-scan assumption, we (iii) extend our framework to the
multi-study setting through three aggregation strategies: semantic retrieval, to-
ken stacking, and embedding averaging. Finally, to rigorously assess clinical relia-
bility, we (iv) implement a multi-dimensional clinical LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation
pipeline measuring anatomical coverage, critical findings, diagnostic plausibility,
and overall report quality, thereby complementing traditional lexical metrics
with clinically grounded fidelity assessment.

Method
Our framework consists of a frozen 3D vision encoder E, a dual-projector align-
ment module, and a medical language model L, trained in two stages following
the visual instruction tuning paradigm [14]. An overview is shown in Fig. 1.
3D Vision Encoding and Dual-Projector Alignment. Given a CT volume
V ∈RD×H×W , we extract volumetric features zv = E(V ) using a pretrained
frozen encoder. We evaluate CT-FM [18], a SegResNet pretrained on 148k CTs
via contrastive learning, and Merlin [3], an I3D ResNet pretrained on 15k ab-
dominal CTs with joint contrastive and EHR supervision.
