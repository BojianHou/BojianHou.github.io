Bojian Hou

AI Research Scientist at Meta
Mountain View, California

Also published as Bo-Jian Hou (侯博建)

I am an AI Research Scientist at Meta in Monetization and Meta Recommendation Systems (MRS). My work centers on LLM-style model architectures and scaling laws for massive-scale ads ranking, including Kunlun (KDD 2026), the architecture behind Meta's Generative Ads Model (GEM), and on representation learning that connects organic and ads signals for ranking and retrieval. I also continue my research on trustworthy large language models, especially calibration, uncertainty estimation, and preference alignment.

Background

I am interested in both the theoretical foundations and the real-world deployment of machine learning, and in building AI systems that are technically rigorous, trustworthy, and socially responsible.

I earned my B.Sc. and Ph.D. in Computer Science at Nanjing University, where my doctoral work with Prof. Zhi-Hua Zhou in the LAMDA Group studied learning in open environments: data arrive as streams, and features appear, disappear, and evolve over time. This line of work appeared at NeurIPS, IJCAI, and AAAI and in TKDE and TNNLS, and received excellent doctoral dissertation awards from Jiangsu Province, JSAI, and Nanjing University.

As a postdoctoral fellow in the Department of Biostatistics, Epidemiology and Informatics at the University of Pennsylvania, advised by Prof. Li Shen, I led a Trustworthy AI research line on fairness, interpretability, calibration, and alignment, from classical models to large language models and often with medical data where errors are costly. I mentored 20+ students there, and our work appeared at NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, and KDD.

At Meta, I bring the same perspective to recommendation and ads ranking at production scale.

Kunlun: Establishing Scaling Laws for Massive-Scale Recommendation Systems through Unified Architecture Design

Bojian Hou, Xiaolong Liu, Xiaoyi Liu, Jiaqi Xu, Yasmine Badr, Mengyue Hang, et al.

KDD 2026 (ADS)

A recommendation architecture designed for predictable power-law scaling: it raises model FLOPs utilization from 17% to 37% on NVIDIA B200 GPUs, doubles scaling efficiency, and is deployed in major Meta Ads models.

Paper arXiv Meta blog

A Semantic-Sampling Framework for Evaluating Calibration in Open-Ended Question Answering

Zhanliang Wang*, Jiancong Xiao*, Ruochen Jin, Shu Yang, Bojian Hou†, Li Shen†

NeurIPS 2026

Sem-ECE evaluates LLM calibration in open-ended QA by sampling answers, grouping them into semantic classes, and using class frequencies as confidence; two estimators are analyzed and tested on five commercial LLMs.

arXiv

Beyond Post-Hoc Temperature Scaling: Bilevel Optimization for LLM Calibration

Ruochen Jin*, Zhanliang Wang*, Zongyu Dai, Jiancong Xiao†, Bojian Hou†

COLM 2026

Replaces post-hoc temperature scaling with a bilevel objective that maximizes predictive entropy, solved by a first-order method that scales to LLMs; calibration gains are strongest out of domain.

arXiv Code Poster
